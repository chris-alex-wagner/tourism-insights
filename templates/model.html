Housing Price Model

Short-term rentals are often blamed for rising housing costs. This page tests a narrower question: can short-term rental activity, together with a few socio-economic indicators, predict how much house prices in a country change from one year to the next?

Data

143 observations – one per country and year (about 25 EU countries, 2018–2023).

  • Target: yearly change of the house price index (in %)
  • Tourism features: stays, nights rented and guest nights, for domestic and foreign guests
  • Socio-economic features: share of population with tertiary education, share of households with internet access
  • Controls: year and country indicators; all features standardised

Method

  1. Split the data: 80 % for training, 20 % held back as an unseen test set (stratified by the target).
  2. Compare four models on the training data with 5-fold cross-validation: Linear Regression, Lasso, Random Forest and Support Vector Regression.
  3. Pick the model with the best average R², tune it and evaluate it once on the test set.
Cross-validation
  Training models … nested cross-validation takes a few seconds longer.
Simple: each model with default settings. Nested: settings (e.g. tree depth) are tuned inside every fold – slower, but a fairer comparison because tuning never sees the fold it is scored on.

1 · Model comparison

R² shows how much of the variation in price changes a model explains: 1 is perfect, 0 is no better than always predicting the average, and below 0 means worse than the average. MAE is the average prediction error in percentage points – lower is better.
